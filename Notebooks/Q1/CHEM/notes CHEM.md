# COLLISION THEORY
**Collision Theory** presupposes that a reaction occurs when reactant molecules
collide with an ==energy== that is greater than some minimum value
and with the ==proper orientation==.
(tldr; ==The more the particles== of two reactants can ==collide==, the ==faster the reaction==.)

![[Pasted image 20260915214809.png|336]]
**activation energy, (E<sub>a</sub>)** is the energy difference between the ==reactants== (initial) and the ==transition state== (highest-energy point)

**Rate constant** - is a measure of how effectively the reactant particles turn collisions into successful reactions under a particular temperature and a set of conditions.
*k = Zpe<sup>-Ea/RT</sup>*
Z = ==frequency== of collisions
p = ==orientation== of collisions
e<sup>-Ea/RT</sup> = fraction of collisions ==having enough energy==

**Orientation**
Only one of the five orientations shown for the collision between NO and NO has the correct orientation to lead to product.

In the effective orientation, contact occurs between the atoms that will become bonded in the product.

# FACTORS AFFECTING RATES OF REACTION
**Surface Area of Reactants**
==Increasing the surface area== of the solid increases the chances of collisions taking place, hence in creasing the number of collisions per second which ==will result in faster rate of reaction==
A *pile of flour* does not burn easily, only the particles on the surface are exposed to oxygen, hence fewer collisions. When it is blown into the air, it forms a cloud of tiny particles with a much larger surface area that is exposed to oxygen, greatly increasing the frequency of collisions
![[Pasted image 20260916120844.png]]
**Effect of Catalyst**
*Catalyst* - a substance that ==increases the rate of reaction== but is not consumed during the reaction
- A *catalyst* speeds up the reactions. A reaction with a catalyst doesn't yield more product than one without it, but it ==yields the product more quickly==
- A *catalyst* ==causes a lower activation energy== by providing a different mechanism for the reaction, and thus a new lower energy pathway.

**Reaction Mechanism**
It is a sequence of single reaction steps that sums up to the overall reaction equation
```
0. 2A + B -> E + F
1.  A + B -> C
2.  A + C -> D
3.      D -> E + F
```
A + B + ~~C~~ + A + ~~D~~ → ~~C~~ + ~~D~~ + E + F
A + B + A → E + F
2A + B → E + F

Steps *1 to 3* are called ==elementary steps==
Elements *C and D* are called ==intermediates==

**Molecularity**
An elementary step is characterized by its *molecularity*, the ==number of reactant particles involved in a step==
![[Pasted image 20260916125453.png]]

**Requirements**
- The sum of elementary steps must give the overall balanced equation for the reaction
- The mechanism must agree with the experimentally determined rate law

**Multistep Mechanisms**
The *slowest elementary step* limits how fast the overall reaction proceeds. Because the rate-determining step limits the rate of the overall reaction, its ==rate law represents the rate law for the overall reaction==
![[Pasted image 20260916130347.png]]

*Slow* initial step - NO<sub>2</sub> (g) + CO (g) -> NO (g) + CO<sub>2</sub> (g)
- The rate law for this equation is found experimentally to be 
  Rate = k[NO<sub>2</sub>]<sup>2</sup>
- *CO* is necessary for this reaction to occur, but the rate of reaction doesn't depend on its concentration
(*1*) NO2 (g) + NO2 (g) → NO (g) + NO [slow]
(*2*) NO3 (g) + CO (g) → NO (g) + CO (g) [fast]
- The NO<sub>3</sub> intermediate is consumed in the second step
- As CO is not involved in the slow rate-determining step, it does not appear in the rate law
(*1*) rate<sub>1</sub> = k<sub>1</sub>[NO<sub>2</sub>] [NO<sub>2</sub>] = k<sub>1</sub>[NO<sub>2</sub>]<sup>2</sup>
(*2*) rate<sub>2</sub> = k<sub>2</sub> [NO3][CO]
- *Note* that if k<sub>1</sub> = k, the rate law for the rate-determining step (step 1) is identical to the experimental rate law. Therefore, rate = k[NO<sub>2</sub>]<sup>2</sup>

*Fast* initial step - 2NO (g) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → 2NO<sub>2</sub> (g)
- The experimentally determined rate law is: Rate = k[NO]<sup>2</sup> [O<sub>2</sub>]
- The proposed mechanism is:
(*1*) NO(g) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → NO<sub>3</sub> (g) [fast]
(*2*) NO<sub>3</sub> (g) + NO (g) → 2NO<sub>2</sub> (g) [slow]
- Step *1* is an equilibrium, it includes the forward and reverse actions
(*1.1*) rate<sub>1 (fwd)</sub> = k<sub>1</sub>[NO][O<sub>2</sub>]
(*1.2*) rate<sub>-1 (rev)</sub> = k<sub>-1</sub>[NO<sub>3</sub>] 
(*2*) rate<sub>2</sub> = k<sub>2</sub>[NO<sub>3</sub>][NO] → *rate for the slow step*
- NO<sub>3</sub> is an intermediate, and an overall rate law can only include reactants
- To get the rate law we express [NO<sub>3</sub>] as a reactant
Rate<sub>1 (fwd)</sub> = Rate<sub>-1 (rev)</sub>
k<sub>1</sub>[NO][O<sub>2</sub>] = k<sub>-1</sub>[NO<sub>3</sub>]
(k<sub>1</sub>[NO][O<sub>2</sub>])/k<sub>-1</sub> = [NO<sub>3</sub>]
![[Pasted image 20260916222859.png]]
- substitute [NO<sub>3</sub>] in the rate law of slow step
Rate<sub>2</sub> = k<sub>2</sub>[NO<sub>3</sub>][NO]
![[Pasted image 20260916222906.png]]
- Hence, Rate = k[NO]<sup>2</sup>[O<sub>2</sub>]

**Average Rate of Reaction**
It is the increase or decrease in molar concentration of product of a reaction per unit time. The unit of rate of reaction is M/s or mol/Ls

Suppose we have a known concentration of O<sub>2</sub> in a closed reaction vessel kept at 30 degrees C 
(303 K). The rate over the entire 60.0s is the total change in concentration divided by change in time:
*Rate = -∆[O<sub>3</sub>]/∆t*
Rate = -[(1.1x10<sup>-5</sup>M) - (3.2x10<sup>-5</sup>M)]/(60s-0s)
<u>Rate = 3.5x10<sup>-7</sup>M/s</u>

**Rate in Terms of Reactant and Product Concentrations**
H<sub>2</sub> (g) + I<sub>2</sub> (g) → 2HI (g)
For every H<sub>2</sub> that disappears, one molecule of I<sub>2</sub> disappears and two molecules of HI appear
