### **Mythology** 
derived from the Greek =="mythos"== meaning "story". The purpose of Mythology is to ==explain natural phenomena== and also contained deep cultural significance to the people. =="Myths teach meaning"==
**Types of Myths**
- *Etiological* - expounds on the ==origin of a place, object, or concept== and/or justifies its inherent nature. =="Aetion"== meaning "reason"
- *Historical* - exaggerated retellings of real events
- *Psychological* - meant to ==resonate with the individual==. The search of one's true identity/ purpose, the resolution of a crisis, and the indoctrination of values that the community upholds.
### **Literature**
has 2 major features:
- *Language* - figurative language
- *Imaginative* character
**Functions**
- To instruct or delight
- To actively shape our culture
- To construct sociocultural realities and;
- To ==involve you, as the reader, in a stimulating interaction with these realities==
**Forms**
1. *Poetry* - The oldest, already existed through ==songs== and ==spoken narratives==, and has identifiable meter, rhyme, and rhythm
	- *Imagery* - words and phrases that communicate sensory experiences and convey moods and emotions
		- *Visual* - :LiEye:
		- *Auditory* - :LiSpeaker:
		- *Olfactory* - :FasPoop:
		- *Gustatory* - :FasFaceGrinTongueSquint:
		- *Tactile* - :FasHandsClapping:
	- *Simile* - a direct comparison between two things. Uses "like"
	- *Metaphor* - an implicit comparison that omits "like" or "as"
	- *Symbol* - when an object or an element of an object is used to convey meaning beyond its original purpose
	- *Personification* - attribution of human qualities to animals, ideas, or inanimate things
	- *Paradox* - a statement that appears to be contradictory and absurd but displays an element of truth
	- *Hyperbole* - exaggeration for the purpose of emphasis
	- *Irony* - implies a distance between what is said and what is meant, and mirrors the authenticity of life
		- *Verbal* - exposes hypocrisy
		- *Dramatic* - builds suspense
		- *Situational* - unexpected outcome
	- *Theme* - lessons or morals that an author wants the reader to learn from a story
2. *Drama* - Typically performed onstage with actors in costumes surrounded by decorations that match the setting and mood intended by the playwriting
3. *Prose* - instead of lines and stanzas like in poetry, it uses sentences and paragraphs
	- *Novel* - often composed of chapters
	- *Short Story* - has a single plot
	- *Biography* - complete retelling of a persons life from birth to death. An "autobiography" is written by the subject themselves
	- *Memoir* - something the author remembers
	- *Essay* - a brief written contemplation of an idea
4. *Oral Literature* - may include ritual texts, curative chants, epic poems, musical genres, folk tales, creation tales, songs, myths, spells, legends, etc.
5. *Proverbs* - sayings
6. *Media* - the most diverse among the forms, and additional guidance and caution must be taken as it is consumed
## Types of Literature
**Narratives**
- *Novel*
- *Short story*
- *Biography*
- *Autobiography*
- *Memoir*
- *Drama*
- *Tale*
- *Myth*
- *Fable*
**Poetry**
- *Haiku* - 5-7-5 syllable count, involves themes like nature
- *Cinquain* - 5 lines
- *Epic* - a long narrative poem about a hero
- *Ballad* - slow love song
- *Lyric* - accompanied with music
- *Sonnet* - 14 lines in iambic (stressed then unstressed syllable) pentameter (5 of them)
**Essay**
- *Narrative*
- *Descriptive*
- *Expository*
- *Critical*
- *Persuasive/Argumentative*
